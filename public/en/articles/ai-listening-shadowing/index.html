“I did not understand it” is too broad. Compare the reference audio, its full transcript, and your recording for one concrete segment.

One goal for this article

Separate the reference, transcript, and your recording before identifying a difference.

A concrete example

Illustrative example, not your study record

Illustrative text only: the reference says そういうわけではないんですが, while you may have heard わけは. This is not a recording transcription.

What a good result looks like

Illustrative result; verify against real data

Keep three fields: reference transcript, what is actually audible in your recording, and the possible missed では. If the recording is unclear, mark it uncertain. Then repeat listening and shadowing in two rounds.

A prompt you can use

Find the listening question and my latest shadowing recording in JLPT Master. Only if audio:read is authorized and you can process both audio files, compare what you actually hear with the full transcript, identify specific differences, and suggest two listening rounds. Mark any inaudible part as uncertain; do not invent a transcription.

Three practical steps

  1. Confirm the exact question, reference audio, full transcript, and learner recording.

  2. Check audio:read permission and whether your client actually received and processed the audio.

  3. Keep observed sounds separate from guesses, then replay the original question.

Where to check the result

Review the analysis with the recording. The full transcript belongs to the shared audio, while per-question notes explain that question.

What to watch for

An audio link is not automatically a transcript. An AI that did not hear the recording cannot claim it analyzed your pronunciation.
