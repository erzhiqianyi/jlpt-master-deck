One goal for this article

Assign time, material range, and a checkable result to each day.

A concrete example

Illustrative example, not your study record

Illustration: N1 preparation, five days per week, 45 minutes a day, grammar textbook completed through lesson 8. Supply your real exam date and page numbers.

What a good result looks like

Illustrative result; verify against real data

A day might contain lesson 9 patterns for 15 minutes, exercises for 20 minutes, and mistake review for 10 minutes. The total is 45 minutes and the output is two recorded confusing patterns plus completed exercises.

A prompt you can use

Read my saved JLPT Master plan, materials, existing tasks, and recent practice. Schedule the next two weeks using my actual exam date, available days, daily minutes, and textbook progress. Give each task a specific lesson or question type, minutes, and expected output. Ask about missing details before saving. Do not rearrange completed tasks.

Three practical steps

  1. Save the exam date, unavailable days, daily study time, and textbook starting point.

  2. Inspect the proposed daily tasks and total minutes.

  3. Open the calendar after saving and spot-check the first week.

Where to check the result

Review the plan calendar. A task should name a lesson, question type, or page and a result you can confirm.

What to watch for

“Study grammar every day” is too vague. Do not fabricate lesson numbers or pages when the textbook information is missing.
