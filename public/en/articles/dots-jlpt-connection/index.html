Connect a dot to keep working on a JLPT weakness

A dot offers more than a run at a fixed time. Give it a goal such as improving N1 shadowing: it can carry earlier decisions forward, adjust the next step as work changes, and return when your judgment is needed.

One goal for this article

Assign one measurable learning goal for ongoing follow-through.

What does a dot add beyond a schedule?

Fixed time

A scheduled task runs a specified workflow at a saved time, such as making a draft every morning at 7.

Ongoing follow-through

A dot keeps the goal and earlier decisions in context, considers new progress, and decides what to investigate next or when to ask you.

Save a schedule if a daily check must happen at a fixed time. Immediate reactions to new answers depend on event support from the data source.

A concrete example

Illustrative example, not your study record

Example: you repeatedly miss ではない in shadowing. On day one, the dot reads relevant practice and recording information. You then say you have only 20 minutes a day this week. When new evidence or feedback becomes available, it adjusts the next listening step.

What a good result looks like

Illustrative result; verify against real data

The dot keeps a short state: goal, evidence actually read, exercise tried, and what the next recording should test. If errors decrease, it explains the evidence and sample size before proposing a new focus. If it cannot access audio, it asks for access or material instead of claiming it listened.

A prompt you can use

Keep improving my N1 shadowing comprehension as an ongoing responsibility. First use my authorized JLPT Master plugin to read recent relevant practice, the full transcript, and any recording information you can actually access. State what you obtained, identify one measurable weakness, and propose the next exercise. When new study records or my feedback become available and you are allowed to continue, compare them with the previous goal and result. Decide whether to keep the same focus, adjust the exercise, or ask me to choose. Contact me only for evidence-backed progress changes, blockers, or decisions. Keep a brief record of the goal and decisions. Do not publish practice or save a daily schedule yet; ask me if a guaranteed daily run is needed.

Three practical steps

  1. Connect and authorize the JLPT Master plugin, then verify one real read. Audio needs its own permission and a client that can process it.

  2. Give the dot a continuing goal and a test for success, such as whether the next recording still misses ではない. Say when you want it to report or ask.

  3. Review its later work in Activity and check cited practice and recordings in JLPT Master. Update your time limits or goal in the same conversation.

Where to check the result

Check the cited listening question and recording history in JLPT Master, and review the dot’s goal, work, and decisions in Activity. You can revise the goal without starting over.

What to watch for

Ongoing follow-through is not real-time monitoring or permission to edit study records. Proactive research only reads permitted data. Fixed-time checks need a saved schedule; event-triggered checks need source support.
